# Evidence reading — how to read publisher trust evidence

okfhub renders trust as **dated-evidence rows**, not badges. This runbook
explains how to read each row. It is the procedural companion to the
[[trust-discipline]] policy — *what* the rows mean.

## The two sections

Every bundle's Trust surface has two clearly-labeled sub-sections (see
[[trust-discipline]] for *why* they are never merged):

1. **Publisher (registry-computed)** — facts the registry computed about the
   source.
2. **Content (from OKF v0.2 frontmatter)** — facts the author asserted about
   the content.

## Publisher rows (registry-computed)

### Structural checks

A set of pass/warn/fail structural-identity checks the build runs on every
bundle: frontmatter parses, `type` field present, ≥1 concept, internal links
resolve, source reachable. These are **identity** facts ("is this a
well-formed OKF bundle"), not quality verdicts. A bundle with all-pass
structural checks is *well-formed*; that says nothing about whether its content
is correct or safe.

### verified-org

Rendered as: *"Publisher `<org>` is a GitHub-verified organization · last
verified <date>"* with a neutral info glyph. **What it means:** the org has
completed GitHub's domain-verification process. **What it does NOT mean:**
"therefore safe" or "therefore high quality." It is provenance context. Note
the carve-out in [[vocabulary-invariant]]: the word "verified" appears *only*
in this GitHub-verified-organization sense, never as a standalone trust label.

### host-popularity

Rendered as a muted line: *"★ N stars · M forks · popularity ≠ safety"*. The
"popularity ≠ safety" disclaimer is **always present**, at every count,
because popularity is a signal of attention, not of safety. The same grey
rendering is used at 0 stars and at 8263 stars. See [[reputation-signals]].

### dns-verified-domain (HTTP-served bundles only)

For bundles published via the HTTP+DNS path (namespace `io.http.<domain>`), the
registry re-derives a deterministic DNS TXT challenge and verifies it against
the domain's authoritative nameservers. Rendered as: *"dns-verified-domain ·
last verified <date>"* (neutral glyph), or *"dns-stale"* if the verification is
older than 30 days. Again — provenance about who controls the domain, not a
content verdict. See [[publish-flow]] for the publish path.

## Content rows (OKF v0.2 frontmatter)

These come from the concept frontmatter itself (see [[what-is-okf]]):

- *"generated by `<actor>` · `<date>"* — the `generated` family. `human:<id>`
  means a human produced it; `agent:<id>` means an agent pipeline did.
- *"verified by `<actor>` · `<date>"* — one row per distinct verifier from the
  `verified` list. A `human:<id>` verifier is the strongest signal available
  (a human reviewed the content); an `agent:<id>` or machine verifier is
  machine-confirmed.
- *"`<N>` concepts with no verification frontmatter"* — the honest absence row.
  Most concepts in the registry today carry no v0.2 frontmatter; they count as
  *unverified*, and the row says so plainly. See [[trust-discipline]]: absent
  families are never rejected, only reported.

### The tier derivation (informational, never a badge)

The spec defines a three-tier derivation per concept: *unverified* (no
`verified`), *machine-confirmed* (only non-human verifiers), *human-reviewed*
(at least one `human:<id>` verifier). This derivation informs **which row to
render**, not a colored badge. There is no green/blue/yellow traffic light —
the tier is a renderer dispatch input, and the visible output is always a
neutral dated-evidence row.

## How to reason about a bundle

1. Read the **publisher** rows to understand provenance: who is the publisher,
   is the source well-formed, does the publisher control the domain/org.
2. Read the **content** rows to understand authorship: who generated the
   concepts, were they reviewed by a human.
3. Decide for yourself. The registry has given you facts; it has not — and will
   not — decide for you.

This is the whole discipline. See [[trust-discipline]] for the *why*,
[[reputation-signals]] for the publisher-signal vocabulary, and
[[vocabulary-invariant]] for the words the registry refuses to use.
